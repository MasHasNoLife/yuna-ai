\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All numbers are LLM-as-judge accuracy (qwen2.5-14b judge, temperature 0) over the
full LoCoMo benchmark (1{,}986 QA pairs). Adversarial (category~5) questions are
scored by abstention detection; because a memory-less model abstains on
everything, that category lifts every strategy's floor, so we report it
separately and never fold it into a single headline number. The answerer model is
held fixed (Gemma4-12B-QAT, Q4, on a single 12\,GB RTX~4070).

\subsection{Memory strategies}
\label{sec:results-strategies}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\toprule
strategy & overall & multi-hop & temporal & open & single-hop & abstain \\
\midrule
none & 0.228 & 0.00 & 0.01 & 0.00 & 0.00 & 1.00 \\
raw\_rag & 0.434 & 0.18 & 0.29 & 0.10 & 0.39 & 0.85 \\
full\_history & \textbf{0.544} & \textbf{0.39} & 0.24 & \textbf{0.22} & \textbf{0.66} & 0.72 \\
agentic (ours) & 0.457 & 0.31 & \textbf{0.37} & 0.16 & 0.35 & 0.88 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Judged accuracy by memory strategy. Agentic memory gives the best
temporal reasoning; full-history wins overall on in-window single-hop copy.}
\label{tab:strategies}
\end{table}

\textbf{Context-stuffing wins on aggregate accuracy} (0.544 vs.\ 0.457 for
agentic memory), driven by single-hop (0.66) and multi-hop (0.39) questions.
When the literal answer sits verbatim inside the window, supplying the raw text
beats supplying a distilled version of it, and we make no attempt to argue
otherwise.

Two findings qualify that result, and the rest of this section develops them.
First, agentic memory gives the \emph{best temporal reasoning of any strategy}
(0.37 vs.\ 0.24), because dates are resolved to absolute form at write time
rather than inferred at read time (\S\ref{sec:results-temporal}). Second,
full\_history's lead depends entirely on the answer surviving truncation---a
condition that holds on this benchmark but weakens as conversations grow
(\S\ref{sec:results-length}).

\subsection{Temporal grounding ablation}
\label{sec:results-temporal}
Absolutizing relative dates at extraction and stamping memories with session
dates is a single isolable mechanism. We run the full benchmark twice with an
identical extractor (Gemma4-12B), toggling only temporal grounding
(Figure~\ref{fig:temporal}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{fig3_temporal_ablation.pdf}
\caption{Temporal grounding ablation. Disabling date absolutization drops
temporal accuracy from 0.36 to 0.08 (4.5$\times$); other categories are
largely unaffected, confirming the mechanism is responsible for the entire
temporal advantage.}
\label{fig:temporal}
\end{figure}

Disabling grounding drops temporal accuracy from 0.364 to 0.081---a
4.5$\times$ reduction---while other categories remain comparable (overall
0.453 vs.\ 0.392). The mechanism is responsible for the entire temporal
advantage at negligible cost: the work is done once at ingest, adding no
latency at answer time.

\subsection{Length-invariance: the core result}
\label{sec:results-length}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{fig1_length_invariance.pdf}
\caption{Retrieval-based memory is length-invariant; context-stuffing collapses
past its window. full\_history's accuracy drops 76\% once a question's evidence
is truncated, while agentic memory is unchanged.}
\label{fig:length}
\end{figure}

full\_history retains only the most recent 60k characters. Splitting the
non-adversarial questions by whether their evidence still lies in that
window (Table~\ref{tab:length}):

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
strategy & evidence in-window ($n{=}927$) & evidence truncated ($n{=}601$) \\
\midrule
full\_history & 0.702 & 0.170 (\textbf{$-$76\%}) \\
agentic (ours) & 0.330 & 0.343 (\textbf{flat}) \\
raw\_rag & 0.306 & 0.329 (flat) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Judged accuracy by whether evidence survives the context window. The
split is reconstructed from LoCoMo evidence turns against the 60k-character
truncation point; see \S\ref{sec:results-robustness} for confidence intervals.}
\label{tab:length}
\end{table}

full\_history's entire advantage comes from questions still inside its window;
beyond it, accuracy collapses 76\%---below either retrieval method. Agentic memory
is length-invariant (0.330 vs.\ 0.343), and outperforms context-stuffing
2.0$\times$ on truncated-evidence questions (Figure~\ref{fig:length}). As a
conversation grows, the truncated fraction approaches 100\%: the regime where
stuffing wins is the one a persistent companion grows out of.

\subsection{Context efficiency}
\label{sec:results-efficiency}
Agentic memory feeds 889 characters of memory per question vs.\ 59{,}585 for
full\_history---\textbf{67$\times$ less context} for competitive accuracy,
fitting a 4k-token window on a 12\,GB GPU.

\subsection{How small can the memory manager be?}
\label{sec:results-ladder}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
extractor & overall & multi-hop & temporal & open & single-hop \\
\midrule
qwen2.5:3b & 0.309 & 0.12 & 0.06 & 0.05 & 0.16 \\
qwen2.5:7b & 0.348 & 0.15 & 0.19 & 0.12 & 0.21 \\
qwen2.5:14b & 0.470 & 0.29 & 0.34 & 0.12 & 0.40 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Judged accuracy by extractor size (answerer fixed). Quality scales with
size; temporal grounding breaks first under compression.}
\label{tab:ladder}
\end{table}

Varying the extractor (answerer fixed), quality scales monotonically with size,
and temporal grounding is the first capability to break: temporal accuracy is
0.06 at 3B, 0.19 at 7B, 0.34 at 14B. A 3B extractor cannot reliably run the typed
protocol on dated information; competence emerges at $\sim$7--14B, setting a
concrete minimum viable size for the memory manager.

This floor is markedly lower than prior experience suggests. MemGPT reports that
even 70B function-calling models failed as memory managers
\citep{packer2023memgpt}; we find a 14B model sufficient. We attribute the gap to
interface width rather than to model capability: emitting five typed operations
into a fixed grammar is a much smaller ask than driving a general function-calling
API over a memory hierarchy, and the parser rejects malformed output rather than
acting on it. Constraining the protocol is what brings the manager within reach
of consumer hardware.

\subsection{Extraction precision}
\label{sec:results-precision}
A hand-verified sample of 200 stored memories, traced to source turns across all
conversations, yields \textbf{199/200 faithfulness precision}: zero
hallucinations and one speaker misattribution (a fact-dense speaker's basketball
history duplicated onto another participant). A stricter usefulness bar lowers
this only to 198/200. We report precision directly and treat downstream QA
accuracy as the recall proxy (missed facts surface as wrong answers); a dedicated
recall annotation with inter-annotator agreement is future work.

\subsection{Statistical robustness}
\label{sec:results-robustness}
We attach non-parametric 95\% confidence intervals (10{,}000-resample
percentile bootstrap over the per-question judged verdicts) to the two central
claims, and test each with a paired bootstrap over the same questions answered
under both conditions.

\emph{Agentic beats context-stuffing on temporal reasoning.} On the 321
temporal questions, agentic scores $0.368$ vs.\ full\_history's $0.237$; the
paired difference is $+0.131$ (95\% CI $[+0.065, +0.196]$, $p<0.001$),
so the temporal advantage is not sampling noise.

\emph{Length-invariance is real.} full\_history's in-window accuracy
($0.702$, CI $[0.67, 0.73]$) and truncated accuracy ($0.170$, CI
$[0.14, 0.20]$) have non-overlapping intervals---the $-76\%$ collapse is
significant. Agentic memory's in-window ($0.330$, CI $[0.30, 0.36]$) and
truncated ($0.343$, CI $[0.30, 0.38]$) intervals overlap almost entirely: it
is \emph{statistically flat} across the window boundary, as
Figure~\ref{fig:length} claims.

\emph{Temporal grounding.} The ablation gap (\S\ref{sec:results-temporal}) is
$+0.283$ on temporal questions (95\% CI $[+0.227, +0.340]$, $p<0.001$). All
three findings survive the bootstrap; the analysis is reproducible from the
frozen judged verdicts via the released \path{robustness_ci.py} script.

\subsection{Cost and interactivity}
Extraction runs asynchronously after the reply streams, so it never delays
time-to-first-token. On the RTX~4070: TTFT $\approx$0.9\,s, generation
$\approx$40+ tok/s, extraction adds $\approx$2\,GB VRAM. The full recall--generate--extract
loop runs in real time on a single consumer GPU.
